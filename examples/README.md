# Mathematical case studies

Each case includes a paper PDF, a short explanation of the mathematical question and its significance, and a link to the corresponding Lean repository.

| Case | Result | Included paper | Formalization |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Lewis–Overton](lewis-overton/) | Initialization-dependent sharp BFGS trial rates on an open parameter interval | [arXiv v1](lewis-overton/paper.pdf), 7 pages | [Lean repository](https://github.com/canghaimeng/lewis-overton-bfgs-rate) |
| [Nikoghosyan](nikoghosyan/) | Hamiltonicity under the original 1-toughness and forbidden-subgraph hypotheses | [Reviewed manuscript, 3 October 2026](nikoghosyan/paper.pdf), 32 pages | [Lean repository](https://github.com/canghaimeng/Nikoghosyan-conjecture) |
| [Jankiewicz–Norin–Wise](jnw/) | A 33-vertex solution of the four-connected legal-system question | [arXiv v1](jnw/paper.pdf), 14 pages | [Lean repository](https://github.com/canghaimeng/JNWLegalSystemObstruction) |

The PDFs are reproduced without changes. The two arXiv papers retain their stated CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 licenses. The separate Lean repositories retain their own licenses and access settings; linking to them does not change either. At this snapshot, Nikoghosyan requires repository access, while the other two Lean repositories are public.

These artifacts document the project's mathematical research history across earlier workflow versions. They do not establish that this release autonomously rediscovered all three results, or constitute a controlled comparison of agent systems. Private team conversations and historical runtime snapshots are not included.
