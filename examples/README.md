# Mathematical case studies

Each case includes a paper PDF, a short explanation of the mathematical question and its significance, links to the corresponding Lean repository, and an [interactive visualization](https://canghaimeng.site/euler/cases/) of the historical research records.

| Case | Result | Included paper | Formalization |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Lewis–Overton](lewis-overton/) | Initialization-dependent sharp BFGS trial rates on an open parameter interval | [arXiv v1](lewis-overton/paper.pdf), 7 pages | [Lean repository](https://github.com/canghaimeng/lewis-overton-bfgs-rate) |
| [Nikoghosyan](nikoghosyan/) | Hamiltonicity under the original 1-toughness and forbidden-subgraph hypotheses | [Reviewed manuscript, 3 October 2026](nikoghosyan/paper.pdf), 32 pages | [Lean repository](https://github.com/canghaimeng/Nikoghosyan-conjecture) |
| [Jankiewicz–Norin–Wise](jnw/) | A 33-vertex solution of the four-connected legal-system question | [arXiv v1](jnw/paper.pdf), 14 pages | [Lean repository](https://github.com/canghaimeng/JNWLegalSystemObstruction) |

The PDFs reproduce the versions listed above. The two arXiv papers use CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Each linked Lean repository supplies its license and build instructions. All three Lean repositories are public.

These cases document the project's mathematical research history across earlier workflow versions. Each case package contains a paper and a guide to its mathematical result and formalization.

The public visualizations preserve proof reading and graph navigation. Team messaging, project creation, restoration, and model calls are disabled.
